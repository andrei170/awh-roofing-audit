Young Lads Are The One Group Advertising Actually Reaches

Prepared for Alex Hickin · 3 October 2026, after our call · AG1 Consulting
What Changed After The Call
This page was aimed at the wrong person and you put us right

Before the call this page was built to attract experienced leadwork and zinc specialists. That was our assumption and it was wrong.

You told us plainly what you want:

“What I need to do is get some lower paid staff. Some young blood, I call them. Younger staff that are willing to get into the industry.”

And why:

“I'm paying good money because I can only find good staff, but then that doesn't always necessarily help. If I've got three skilled workers on one job, that's not really required. I could have three skilled workers on three separate jobs, but what they need is a helping hand.”

⭐⭐ That changes the whole approach, and it changes it in your favour. Reaching an established forty-year-old roofer with fifteen years behind him is genuinely hard, because he is not looking and not listening. Reaching an eighteen-year-old who does not know what he wants to do yet is the single easiest audience there is.

Your Own Objection, Answered Properly
You said it and it is the fair challenge

“The younger generation are just not that keen to do the roofing. Roofing is quite a hard industry. They come in and they expect to be paid an absolute fortune. And they don't know anything.”

All three of those are true. None of them is a reason this cannot work. They are a description of what the advert has to do:

  • “Not keen” is a volume problem, not a wall. If one lad in twenty wants outdoor graft and a trade, you do not need to change the other nineteen. ⭐ You need to reach enough of them that the one shows up, and that is exactly what advertising is for.
  • “Expect a fortune” is fixed by saying the money up front. ⭐⭐ The single most effective filter on a trade advert is stating the pay. Anybody expecting a fortune reads it and does not apply, which saves you the phone call. Anybody who applies anyway has already accepted it.
  • “They don't know anything” is the point. You are not hiring knowledge, you are hiring attitude and a pair of hands. ⚠️ So the advert should promise hard work, not opportunity — cold mornings, heights, lifting, learning properly from men who have done it twenty years. The ones that puts off were never going to last.

⛔ What we will not do is write a soft advert that gets you forty applicants and no roofers. The honest version gets fewer and better, which is the same principle you agreed to for the repair enquiries.

🔴 The Thing That Has To Be Settled Before Anything Else
🔴🔴 You do not employ anybody. An apprentice usually has to be employed

You were clear on the call:

“All of them, really. I don't employ it. I'm just not established enough to. It doesn't work like that in this industry. Roofing is one of those sort of, it's a subcontracting industry.”

⚠️ That works for a skilled man with his own van, his own tools and his own insurance. It is a different matter for a nineteen-year-old with no experience. Somebody being trained, supervised and told what to do all day is not usually a subcontractor, whatever the paperwork says, and getting that wrong sits with you rather than with him.

⛔ We are a marketing company and this is not our call to make. We are not going to advise you on employment status, CIS or apprenticeship rules, and you should not take advice on it from us.

⭐ But it decides what we advertise, so it has to be answered first. There are broadly three routes and they produce three different adverts:

  • Labourer on subcontract — a general pair of hands, not formally being trained. Closest to how you already work.
  • Employed apprentice or trainee — proper training, your name on it, and payroll you have said you do not want.
  • A formal apprenticeship scheme through CITB or a college, where a good deal of the structure and some of the cost sits outside your business.

Ask your accountant which of those you are willing to do, and we will write to that. ⚠️ Tracy may well have a view too.

Where We Would Look, In Order
  • 1. Paid social, and this time it is genuinely the right channel. ⭐⭐ Seventeen to twenty-two year olds are on Instagram, TikTok and Facebook constantly, and they can be targeted by age and area very precisely. This is the opposite of trying to reach a working tradesman — the audience is reachable, cheap and enormous. It is also why the concept page matters: the advert's only job is to get them to the page, and the page does the work.
  • 2. Say the money. The biggest single factor in whether this produces useful applicants or nonsense. ⚠️ Your call whether it is a figure or a range, but blank is the worst option.
  • 3. Colleges and training providers in your patch. ⭐ There are young people already on construction and roofing courses who need placements and real work. They have self-selected into the trade, which is the hardest part already done. Costs almost nothing but a few phone calls, and we cannot make them for you.
  • 4. Your own crew, with money attached. ⭐ A referral payment to your six or seven men. They know who has a younger brother, a cousin, a lad down the road. Usually beats anything we can run, and you are already doing a version of it by asking Tracy's niece.
  • 5. Keep the Eugene conversation going. You mentioned the WhatsApp contact who can provide labourers and said you had not followed through. ⚠️ If he produces three next week, none of this is needed, and we would rather you got your lads cheaply than paid us.
  • 6. The page stays up. You asked and the answer is yes — awhroofing.co.uk/hiring is yours, it stays when a campaign stops, and it can be switched back on whenever you need somebody.
What The Page Has To Answer, And What We Need From You
⭐ What makes a lad apply
  • What he will actually be doing day to day. Not a job title.
  • The money, or a range.
  • Where, and how he gets there.
  • That he will be taught properly. ⭐ You served a traditional apprenticeship and trained with the Lead Sheet Association — that is a genuine reason to come to you rather than a firm that will leave him sweeping up.
  • That he deals with the owner. You are on site at least half the week. Say so.
  • Whether there is a future in it. Lead work is a skilled trade that pays.
⛔ What we cannot fill in
  • Transport. Does he need his own vehicle or can he meet the crew? ⚠️ This single answer changes who we can reach more than anything else on the page.
  • Tools, boots and hard hat — yours or his?
  • A CSCS card or any ticket, or will you take somebody with nothing?
  • How he applies. ⚠️ You are on a roof half the week. If twenty-five people apply, does your mobile ring, or does it come in as a form you read of an evening? And who rings them back?
  • When you need them, and whether the Hemel Hempstead work is the trigger.
What We Are Still Not
⛔ Unchanged from before the call

AG1 is a marketing company, not a recruitment agency. We will not interview anybody, assess anybody, check a reference, or tell you who to take on. We cannot guarantee a hire, a number of applicants, or that anybody who applies will be any use.

⭐ What we can do is make sure the right lads see that you are looking, and that the page answers their questions before they ring you. The deciding stays entirely with you.

An established roofer is almost unreachable. A seventeen year old is the easiest audience there is.

Which is lucky, because that is who you actually need.
