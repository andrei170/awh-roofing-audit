We Cannot Hire For You. We Can Change Who Applies.

Prepared for Alex Hickin · 3 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
What We Are Not
⛔ Said first, because the rest is worthless if this is not clear

AG1 is a marketing company. We are not a recruitment agency and we are not going to behave like one.

  • ❌ We will not interview anybody, assess a roofer's competence, or tell you who to take on.
  • ❌ We cannot verify a trade qualification or check a reference.
  • ❌ We cannot guarantee a hire, a number of applicants, or that anyone who applies will be any good. Anybody promising you that is guessing.
  • ❌ We cannot make a skilled leadworker exist in Essex if there is not one looking.

⭐ What recruitment advertising actually is: getting the right people to find out you are hiring, and getting them to want the job. That is an audience and messaging problem, and it is the same discipline as everything else we do. The deciding and the hiring stay entirely with you.

Why A Job Advert Has Probably Not Worked
The people you want are not looking for a job

A good leadworker or zinc roofer with fifteen years behind them is already employed. They are not browsing Indeed on a Tuesday evening. So any channel built around people actively searching for work reaches the wrong half of the trade by design.

⚠️ Which also explains the quality complaint. If the only people who see your advert are the people currently out of work, you are selecting from the part of the trade that is available rather than the part that is good. That is not bad luck, it is the channel.

⭐ Reaching a working tradesman means interrupting them where they already are, not waiting where jobseekers go. In practice that means social feeds, trade groups, word of mouth and the merchant counter — not a job board.

The Thing You Already Own And Are Not Using
⭐⭐ Your work is genuinely interesting to a good roofer. Nobody can see that

Think about what a skilled roofer wants from a firm, beyond money: interesting work, somebody who knows the trade properly, and not being treated as a pair of hands.

You have all three and it is not written down anywhere:

  • Leadwork, zinc and sheet metal. ⭐ A tradesman who cares about the craft would rather do that than strip and re-tile estates all year.
  • An owner who served a traditional apprenticeship, studied with the Lead Sheet Association, and trained in New Zealand and Belgium. Good people want to work for somebody they can learn from.
  • Named, specified, systems-based projects rather than anonymous repairs.

🔴 Now look at what a roofer sees if they google you. No careers page. No team page with faces or names. No photographs of the crew working. No statement of what it is like to work there. The most attractive thing about the job is completely invisible to the person you want to attract.

⭐ That is a page, not a campaign, and it would be the first thing we built.

Where We Would Actually Look, In Order Of Likelihood
  • 1. Your own crew, with money attached. ⭐⭐ A referral bounty paid to your existing people almost always beats any advertising we could run, and we would say so before taking a penny for ads. Good tradesmen know other good tradesmen, they know who is unhappy where, and a recommendation from somebody already on your books is pre-vetted in a way no advert can be. ⚠️ Make it enough to matter and pay it on arrival as well as on retention.
  • 2. A careers page that sells the craft. The specialism, the training, the projects, what you actually pay, and what a week looks like. ⭐ It is where everything else points, so it comes before any advertising spend.
  • 3. Paid social, targeted geographically and by trade. This is the part that is properly ours. Reaching working roofers in Essex, Hertfordshire and the surrounding area in their own feed. ⚠️ It will also reach unsuitable people — the filtering happens in the message and in your process, not in the targeting.
  • 4. Trade and local groups where roofers already talk to each other. ⭐ Cheap, slow, and credible — and it works better when it does not look like an advert.
  • 5. Merchants and suppliers. ⭐ You buy lead, zinc and specialist systems from somewhere, and the counter staff know who is any good and who is looking. This costs nothing and we cannot do it for you.
  • 6. Apprenticeships and growing your own. ⚠️ Slow, and it does not solve this year. But you came into the trade through a traditional apprenticeship, and a firm doing leadwork and zinc is one of the few that can genuinely train somebody worth having. If the shortage is structural, this is the only permanent answer.
The Three Things That Decide This, And None Of Them Are Marketing
⚠️ If the answer to any of these is wrong, no amount of advertising fixes it
  • Pay. If you are at or below the local rate for a skilled man, better advertising produces better candidates who then decline. ⛔ We would be wasting your money and we would tell you.
  • Travel and patch. Essex, Hertfordshire and London is a wide area. A roofer in Romford and a job in Hertfordshire is a real daily cost to them, and it narrows the pool more than people expect.
  • What the job actually is. ⭐ If the work is mostly general replacement with occasional leadwork, you are competing with every other roofer on wage alone. If it is genuinely specialist, you are competing with very few people and the craft is your advantage. This is the same question as the job value page, and it has the same answer.

⭐⭐ Which is the useful connection: the work that pays best is also the work that attracts the best people. Moving up your own range is a hiring strategy as much as a revenue one. The two problems you have are closer to being one problem than they look.

What We Would Need From You
  • 1. What are you actually paying, and how does that sit against the local rate? ⛔ We cannot advise honestly without this.
  • 2. Employed or subcontract? They attract completely different people and the advert is not the same.
  • 3. How many, and for what? One leadworker is a different job from three general roofers.
  • 4. What have you already tried? Job boards, agencies, word of mouth — and what came back.
  • 5. Would you take on an apprentice? ⚠️ It does not help this year and it may be the only thing that helps in three.
  • 6. Why did the last person who left, leave? ⭐ Uncomfortable, and the most informative question on the page.

A good roofer wants interesting work and somebody to learn from. You have both.

There is not one word about it anywhere they would look.
