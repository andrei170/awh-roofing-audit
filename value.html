Same Crew, Same Number Of Jobs, A Different End Of Your Own Range

Prepared for Alex Hickin · 3 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
The Arithmetic You Are Sitting On
Two numbers decide your turnover, and you can only move one of them

Turnover is jobs multiplied by average job value. That is the whole of it.

  • Jobs is capped by your crew, and you have told us the crew is the constraint and you cannot easily add to it.
  • Average job value is not capped by anything except which enquiries you attract and which ones you accept.

⭐⭐ So for you, specifically, average job value is not one lever among several. It is the only one currently available.

And your range is £20,000 to £100,000. A five-fold spread is unusually wide, and wide is good news here: it means the top of your range is work you already know how to do. We are not asking you to learn a new trade. We are asking which jobs arrive.

Why Your £100k Work Is Harder To Attract Than Your £20k Work
And it is not because the clients are fussier

A £20,000 roof replacement is bought by a homeowner who searches, reads three sites, gets three quotes and picks one. Your website is adequate for that buyer today.

A £100,000 zinc or leadwork job is bought differently:

  • It is often specified rather than shopped — an architect, a conservation officer, a surveyor or a main contractor names who can do it.
  • The buyer needs evidence of comparable work, not reassurance of general competence. They want to see a zinc roof you have actually built.
  • Accreditation and insurance-backed warranty get asked about early, often as a tick-box before you are even invited to quote.
  • The decision takes weeks or months, so there is time for a slow, well-documented website to do the work — which is the opposite of the repair market.

🔴 Measured against that list, your site is doing almost none of the job. The leadwork is behind a general offer, the four projects carry no scope or value, no accreditation appears anywhere, and nothing is measuring whether a specifier ever lands on the page.

What Would Actually Have To Change
⭐ Attract the top of the range
  • Lead it with the specialism. Leadwork, zinc and sheet metal on the homepage, not three clicks in. The general work can stay; it just stops being the headline.
  • Turn the four projects into real case studies — the problem, what you specified, why that system, roughly what it cost, how long, and what the client said.
  • A page for each specialism that a specifier could send to a client as evidence.
  • Put every accreditation you hold where it can be seen — and if the LCA is not held, have a view on whether it is worth holding.
  • A route that is not your mobile for enquiries that are not urgent repairs.
⛔ And deter the bottom of it
  • Say what you do not do. A line declining small repairs removes more wasted quoting than any filter we could build.
  • Ask about scope on the form before you ask for a phone number. You already ask for a postcode and a description — this is a small extension of something that works.
  • Stop competing for the repair search. It is the cheapest traffic to attract and the worst use of your week.
  • ⚠️ Be clear that this costs you work on purpose. Fewer enquiries, better ones. If that feels wrong, say so on the call, because it is the whole strategy and it should not be a surprise later.
The Honest Objection, Raised By Us Rather Than You
“Bigger jobs tie up my crew for longer, so I do fewer of them”

That is correct and it is the right challenge. A £100k roof might occupy the crew for the time of three £30k roofs, which would make the arithmetic above a wash.

⭐ But it is usually not a wash, for three reasons worth testing against your own numbers:

  • Fixed costs per job do not scale with job size. Quoting, surveying, scheduling, invoicing, chasing and mobilising cost roughly the same on a £25k roof as a £90k one. Fewer, larger jobs means less of your week spent not roofing.
  • Specialist work generally carries a better margin, because fewer firms can do it and it is less often decided on price alone. ⚠️ Whether that is true for you is something only you can confirm.
  • Your time is the scarcest thing in the business. Twenty jobs a year is twenty sets of client phone calls. Twelve larger ones is twelve.

⛔ If your own figures say the margin on a big zinc job is no better than on a straightforward replacement, this whole page is wrong and we would want to know on Saturday rather than three months in.

The One Thing That Has To Happen First
🔴 None of the above can be judged while nothing is counting

There is no analytics on your site. ⭐ So if we changed everything on this page, neither of us could tell you whether it worked.

Measurement is cheap, it takes very little time, and it is the difference between a plan and an opinion. It goes first regardless of what else is agreed, and it is worth doing even if you decide to work with nobody at all.

You cannot do more jobs. You can do the same jobs at the other end of your own range.

Twenty-five to forty thousand average, on twenty jobs, is three hundred thousand pounds and not one extra hire.
