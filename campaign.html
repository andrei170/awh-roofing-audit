Roughly one person in ten in England is aged 16 to 24, which would put the young people in those four districts at around 45,000. That is our estimate from the totals, not a counted figure.

You need two or three of them.

One rule that shapes the advert

Job adverts on Facebook and Instagram cannot be aimed by age. Every job advert goes to all adults in the area. So the advert's wording and pictures do the aiming: "no experience needed", "learn a trade", a real job, a real boss.

And the advert itself should not state an age, because UK law does not let you advertise a job for a particular age group.

How to read that
  • It is a planning model, not a forecast. Nobody has run a hiring advert for you before, so there is no real number for your area yet
  • You know the real figure in week one. If an application is costing more than the careful column, we change the advert or stop

The Three Adverts We Would Start With
A real photo of one of your jobs
Bray, Hale End Road, Maldon Lane or Middleway
1. "No experience needed. Learn roofing properly."

Says the pay, says it is hard work, says who you would be learning from. The plain one, and usually the one that works.

30 seconds of you, on a phone, on site
2. You, to camera

"I'm Alex. I've run AWH Roofing since 2007. I'm looking for somebody who wants to learn the trade." People apply to a person, not a logo.

Four pictures: the start, the lift, the leadwork, the finished roof
3. "What a week looks like"

The early start, the carrying, the bit you learn, the finished job. Puts off the ones who would not last, which saves you the phone call.

All three send people to one place: the hiring page on your own website, with the two-minute form. See the hiring page demo.

The Plan, Week By Week
Before anything is built: four answers from you
  • The arrangement: subcontract labourer, employed trainee, or apprenticeship. Your accountant's call
  • The pay. A real figure. It is the best filter there is
  • How they get to site: their own transport, or a pick-up
  • Whether they need a driving licence
Week 1: build
  • The hiring page goes onto awhroofing.co.uk/hiring, with Tracey, so it sits properly in the site she built
  • Tracking installed, so every application shows where it came from
  • The form set to reach your phone straight away
Weeks 2 to 5: the advert runs
  • Three adverts live, at about £10 to £20 a day, in your own account
  • Reply to each application the same day. A five-minute phone chat, then a paid trial day for the ones who sound right
  • A short update from us every week: applications, cost of each, and what we changed
  • The weakest advert is replaced each week
End of the month: switch off
  • The advert stops. The page stays, and it is yours
  • One page from us: what it cost, how many applied, how many you saw, how many you took on
The free routes, run alongside. Any of these may beat the advert
  • A referral payment to your own six or seven men. They know who has a younger brother or a mate looking
  • Eugene, the WhatsApp contact for labourers you have not followed up yet
  • Tracey's niece, who you have already asked
  • Local colleges with construction courses, such as Harlow College and Hertford Regional College. Their students need placements. Worth a phone call, to confirm what each runs

If one of those finds your three, do not pay us. The hiring page is still useful, because it is where you send them.

One month of adverts, in the area you actually want to work in. Then off.

You said you want it to tick over, not keep growing. This is built to stop.
