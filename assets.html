What Changed, And Why
Two of these pages were built on the wrong assumption. You corrected both
  • 🔴 The hiring page was aimed at experienced leadwork and zinc roofers. You want young lads who will learn — a second pair of hands so a skilled man is not stood next to two others on the same roof. Rewritten.
  • 🔴 The money page argued you should chase bigger jobs. You never said that. Your argument is two or three jobs running at once instead of one, and the cost is cash tied up in materials on part-finished roofs. Replaced.
  • ⭐ And zinc came off the table. You put zinc and asphalt out to your own specialists. Lead is the specialism that is yours, and the flat systems are bringing the enquiries.

The price is on its own page, What It Costs, so the documents above stay about the problem.

Everything In One Place
  • Two Jobs At Once — why a labourer per crew is the lever, the six numbers we would want from you, and three honest limits. Start here.
  • Hiring — rewritten for apprentices and labourers. Your own objection about young people answered properly, where we would look, and 🔴 the subcontract question that has to be settled first.
  • Pre-Call Audit — the website findings, what you answered on the call, and the eight things still open.
  • Competitors - six specialist roofers around you, read from their own sites: who shows which trust marks, who names projects, and why none of them is visibly hiring.
  • What It Costs - one payment, nothing monthly, and exactly what is and is not included.
  • Recruitment Page Concept — the page you saw on the call. ⭐ Now rewritten for people who want to learn the trade, as you asked. A design proposal, not a live site.
The Short Version
⭐ What is working and should be left alone

People ring you having searched Res-Tec and Protan and landed on those pages. ⭐⭐ That was the most valuable thing you said on the call — your system pages are already producing work. Named projects (Bray, Hale End Road, Maldon Lane, Middleway), named systems (Protan, Res-Tec, RubberBond), service pages, a working enquiry form, and a stated service area.

⭐ Plus the things that sell the job to a young lad: a traditional apprenticeship, Lead Sheet Association training, and a boss who is on site at least half the week rather than an agency.

🔴 1. No measurement of any kind — cheapest fix, goes first

No analytics, no tag manager, no pixel. Four project write-ups and no way to know whether anybody has read one. ⛔ And nothing can be judged after a campaign without it.

🔴 2. The old listings need cleaning up — you raised this

AWH sole trader, AWH Limited, Prospect Roofing and your mother's old Kingsman address all still showing. Somebody checking you out before a big job should find one business at one address.

🔴 3. The site never says where you are, where it counts — an hour

Your service area is in the page text, which is good. But there is no local business markup, no postal address, no postcode and no coordinates in the code — which is what search engines read.

⚠️ 4. Your sitemap lists two pages. You have around seventeen

Nothing is pointing Google at the system pages that are already bringing you work.

⚠️ 5. The project write-ups and the old testimonials

The four projects carry no scope, no value and no client voice. And you had testimonials on the previous site that were dropped in the rebuild. ⭐ Both work on customers and on a lad deciding whether this is a proper firm.

The Two Questions That Decide Everything Else
1. Subcontract or employed? 🔴 You employ nobody, and a lad being trained and supervised all day usually has to be employed. That is for your accountant, not for us, but it changes what we advertise and it changes what the page says.

2. Does he need his own transport? ⭐ A nineteen-year-old in Bishop's Stortford without a car is a completely different pool from one who drives. It changes who we can reach more than anything else on the list.
