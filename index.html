You Said The Problem Has Never Been Quantity. We Agree.

Prepared for Alex Hickin · updated 3 October 2026 after our call · AG1 Consulting
Why We Are Not Going To Pitch You More Leads
You told our team the bottleneck, and it is not enquiries

What came back from that conversation was this: you do not have the staffing to handle the jobs you already have, let alone more of them. In your words, the problem has never been quantity.

⛔ So an agency turning up to sell you a bigger pipeline is selling you a worse week. If we filled your inbox tomorrow you would be quoting work you cannot resource, and the honest outcome of that is slower quotes, later starts and a reputation you spent nineteen years building taking the hit.

⭐ There is a different lever, and it is the one your own numbers point at. You told us your jobs run from £20,000 to £100,000. That is a five-fold spread. With the same crew and the same number of jobs, which end of that range you work at is worth more than any amount of extra enquiry.

That is the entire conversation we want to have, and it is set out on the job value page.

First, A Correction To What We Were Told
⚠️ You were described to us as having no online presence. That is not accurate

We read your site before writing this. You have a considerably better website than we were led to expect, and it would be dishonest to open a call pretending otherwise:

  • Four named project write-ups — Bray Refurbishment, Hale End Road, Maldon Lane, Middleway Roof Refurbishment. ⭐ Most roofers have a gallery of photographs. You have named projects.
  • Dedicated pages for three specified systems — Protan, Res-Tec, RubberBond.
  • Separate service pages for flat roofs, leadwork, pitched roofs and repairs.
  • A working enquiry form that asks for a postcode and a description of the work.
  • A clear service area — Essex, Hertfordshire, London and surrounding counties.

Whoever built that understood your trade. The problems below are real, but “you have nothing” is not one of them, and we would rather say so than flatter a finding.

What Your Own Site Says About You, Which Is The Interesting Part
⭐ This is not a commodity roofing business and the site proves it

Reading your About section back to you, because it is the most valuable thing you own and we suspect you undersell it:

  • Founded March 2007 by you.
  • Traditional apprenticeship, then specialist studies with the Lead Sheet Association.
  • Worked in New Zealand, learning corrugated and sheet metal roofing.
  • Returned to the UK in 2006 and trained in Belgium in European zinc roofing techniques.
  • Described on the site as “specialising in expert leadwork and advanced roofing systems”.

⚠️ Corrected after the call: you told us zinc and asphalt go out to your own specialists and you add your margin, with asphalt perhaps one job a quarter. So lead is the specialism that is genuinely yours, and the flat systems are what brings the enquiries.

⭐⭐ Leadwork is not what a general roofer does. It is heritage and architectural work, it gets specified rather than shopped on price, and you said yourself that central London “comes with a lot of the high-end lead work” — which is why you will not write London off despite the travel.

⚠️ And yet your homepage leads with “all aspects of roof replacement and repair”. The specialism is in there, but it is behind the general offer, which means the cheapest enquiries reach you just as easily as the best ones. You agreed on the call that the cheap emergency repair calls are not worth your time, so saying that on the site turns them away before they ring.

The Real Problems, Hardest First
🔴 1. There is no measurement on your website whatsoever

We checked for every standard tool. No Google Analytics, no Tag Manager, no advertising tag, no pixel. Nothing.

So you cannot currently answer: how many people visit, which pages they read, whether anyone looks at the Maldon Lane write-up you paid to have produced, or how many of those visitors filled in the form. You have four project case studies and no way of knowing whether a single person has read one.

⭐ This is the cheapest thing on the list and it has to come first, because every other decision below is a guess until something is counting.

🔴 2. No trade accreditation appears anywhere on the site — and for your specialism that is a real gap

We found no mention of NFRC, CompetentRoofer, CHAS, Constructionline, TrustMark, Checkatrade or any other scheme.

🔴 Most pointedly, there is no Lead Contractors Association membership. Your site says you studied with the Lead Sheet Association, which is training. The LCA is the thing specifiers actually look for on leadwork, and it carries a workmanship warranty.

⭐ If you already hold any of these and they are simply not on the site, that is a ten-minute fix worth more than a month of advertising. If you hold none of them, that is a genuine question about whether the top of your range is reachable, because architect-specified and insurance-backed work tends to require them. We are asking, not assuming.

🔴 3. Your website never says where you are, in the place that counts

Your page text does say your service area — “Essex, Hertfordshire, London and the surrounding counties” — and that is good. ⭐ So this is not the usual complaint that nobody can tell where you work.

🔴 But there is no local business information in your site's code at all. We checked: no business address, no town, no postcode, no opening hours, no map coordinates, and no local business markup of any kind. There is no postal address published anywhere on the site in any form.

⚠️ Search engines read that code to decide which businesses to show for “roofer near me”. Prose in a paragraph is not the same thing and does not do the same job. ⭐ For comparison, another firm we audited this week has its address, hours and coordinates all correctly published — and it is a smaller operation than yours.

⭐ This is a technical fix of an hour or two with no ongoing cost, and for a business whose work is local and whose jobs are large, it is one of the better-value items on the list.

⚠️ 4. A mobile number is your only published contact route

07889 140051 is the one number on the site. No landline.

For a homeowner, that is fine. ⚠️ For a £100,000 commercial or architect-specified job it reads as a smaller operation than you are, and it means every enquiry — a £400 repair and a £100k zinc roof alike — rings the same phone, which is almost certainly in your pocket while you are on a roof.

⭐ You do have alex@ and info@ on your own domain, which is better than most. The routing is the issue, not the addresses.

⚠️ 5. Your sitemap lists two pages. Your website has around seventeen

A sitemap is the file that tells Google what exists. Yours declares two addresses, while your navigation offers roughly seventeen — every service page, all four projects, all three roofing systems.

That does not make those pages invisible, but it does mean nothing is actively pointing Google at them. The most valuable content you own is the part least likely to be indexed.

⚠️ 6. The four project write-ups carry no money, no scope and no client voice

They are named projects, which already puts you ahead. But a specifier comparing contractors wants to know what was wrong, what you specified, what it cost roughly, how long it took and what the client said.

⭐ You almost certainly have all of that in your own records. Turning four write-ups into four real case studies is a writing job, not a marketing spend, and it is the single highest-value thing that could be done to the site as it stands.

The Staffing Problem, Said Honestly
⛔ We are not a recruiter, and we will not pretend to be one

You said you cannot get quality people. We cannot interview, vet, train or guarantee a roofer, and any agency that implies otherwise is selling you something it cannot deliver.

⭐ What we can genuinely do is the advertising half of recruitment — which is a real discipline and is closer to our actual trade than most people realise. Skilled leadwork and zinc people are findable, they are not reading job boards, and reaching them is an audience-targeting problem.

That is set out properly on the hiring page, including what it cannot do.

⚠️ And there is an uncomfortable possibility worth naming now: if you genuinely cannot resource more work and cannot hire, then the only remaining growth lever is price and job selection — doing the same number of jobs at the top of your range. That is not a consolation prize. On a £20k–£100k spread it is the largest lever you have.

Answered On The Call — No Need To Go Over It Again
⭐ What we were guessing at before, and what you actually told us
  • Accreditation. No NFRC and no Lead Contractors Association. You are a member of the Lead Sheet Association and accredited on the flat roofing systems. The CompetentRoofer application is sat half-finished because of the paperwork. ⚠️ And insurance-backed guarantees have become difficult because of a leak on a job about ten years ago under Prospect Roofing.
  • The crew. Six or seven skilled men, all subcontract. You employ nobody directly, because the trade works that way.
  • The specialism. Lead is yours. Zinc and asphalt go out to trusted specialists and you add your margin, and asphalt is perhaps one job a quarter. ⭐ The flat systems are what is actually bringing work in — RubberBond, Protan, Res-Tec and Flexitech.
  • Where the work comes from. Word of mouth, and people searching specific systems and landing on your pages. ⭐⭐ You said people ring you out of the blue having read the Res-Tec and Protan pages. That is the most valuable sentence of the call and it tells us those pages are earning their keep.
  • The patch. Inside the M25 and a little north, based in Bishop's Stortford. Sick of the London travel but not writing London off, because that is where the high-end lead work is.
  • How you run it. A working boss, on site at least half the week, doing your own quotes and dealing with clients directly.
  • The company history. AWH sole trader, then AWH Limited, then Prospect Roofing, now AWH Roofing Ltd. ⭐ Which explains the stale listings below, and you raised it before we did.
🔴 And one finding that only emerged because you mentioned it

You said old versions of the business are still floating about online — AWH sole trader, AWH Limited, Prospect Roofing, and your mother's old address at Kingsman still showing as AWH Roofing.

Your words: “It'd be nice to get them removed or understand why they stay there or clean that up, for sure.”

⭐ Agreed, and it matters more than it sounds. Somebody checking you out before a large job should find one business at one address, not three businesses and a house you have no connection to. It is also the sort of thing that quietly undermines a quote without anybody telling you why.

What Is Still Open
  • 1. Transport. Does a lad need his own van or car, or can he meet the crew? ⭐ This decides who we can realistically reach more than anything else.
  • 2. Kit. Tools, boots, hard hat — yours or his?
  • 3. Any ticket needed? A CSCS card or similar for your sites, or will you take somebody with nothing?
  • 4. How should applicants reach you? ⚠️ You are on a roof half the week. If twenty-five people apply, does your mobile ring, or does it come in as a form you read of an evening — and who rings them back, you or Tracy?
  • 5. Hemel Hempstead. Is that work still live, and roughly when would you need bodies for it? It decides whether this is urgent or a January job.
  • 6. The pay line. Not what you pay anyone. Just whether you want a figure on the page, a range, or nothing. ⚠️ Blank is the worst of the three.
  • 7. 🔴 Subcontract or employed. You employ nobody, and a lad being trained and supervised usually has to be employed. That is a question for your accountant, not for us, but it changes what we advertise. Set out properly here.
  • 8. Did Eugene come back to you? If he produces three labourers, none of this is needed.
Question one is the one that changes the advert, and question seven is the one that changes the job. ⭐ Everything else on this page can be got on with regardless of the answers.
